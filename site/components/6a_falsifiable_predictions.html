6. Falsifiable Predictions

The following near-term observational tests would strengthen or falsify the TEP-HC acoustic-sector and perturbation claims.

  1. Small-scale CMB polarization from active scalar perturbations. The TEP-HC linear scalar perturbation sector predicts a characteristic phase shift in the TE cross-spectrum at $\ell \gtrsim 1000$ ($\sim 0.1\%$–$0.3\%$) due to the $\alpha_B \neq 0$ running of the conformal scalar. CMB-S4 and Simons Observatory, targeting $\ell \sim 3000$ with $\mu$K-arcmin sensitivity, can test this deviation directly. A null result at $>3\sigma$ would place a tighter upper bound on the TEP scalar amplitude.
  2. B-mode polarization from native tensor modes. The TEP-TH tensor analysis yields $r(k_{\rm pivot}) = 1.9 \times 10^{-9}$ and $r_{\rm max} = 5.2 \times 10^{-7}$ at the fiducial profile ($p=0.1$), bounded by $r_{\rm max} = 1.4 \times 10^{-5}$ on the curvature-regular branch ($0 \lt p \le \tfrac12$) and $r_{\rm max} = 6.1 \times 10^{-5}$ over every tested profile ($0 \lt p \le 1$) — all far below current BICEP/Keck bounds ($r < 0.036$). A primordial B-mode detection with $r \gtrsim 10^{-4}$ would falsify the temporal-horizon tensor sector for every tested profile; a detection in the window $1.4\times10^{-5} \lesssim r \lesssim 6\times10^{-5}$ would be accessible only to the curvature-excluded steep branch ($p \gt \tfrac12$) and is likewise excluded. CMB-S4's $r \sim 10^{-4}$ sensitivity reaches the full predicted range, so a null result there remains compatible and constrains the transition-profile parameters.
  3. Acoustic-peak consistency under tep_mode background. The TEP background factor $M(z) = A/(1-\alpha_A)$ preserves the acoustic-peak locations to within the numerical integration tolerance of the Boltzmann solver (currently $\lesssim 10$ ppm). The current hi_class implementation reports $r_s^{\rm TEP}/r_s^{\Lambda\rm CDM} = 0.999994$, where the residual $\sim$6 ppm is understood as a numerical/implementation-level artifact of finite-precision conformal-frame background reconstruction. Any claimed physical deviation from the exact conformal isomorphism must be shown to exceed the standard solver interpolation noise ($\sim 10$ ppm) before it can be interpreted as a falsification of the background mapping, rather than as a C++ integration artifact.
  4. Growth-factor amplitude from native EFT. The TEP-HC active-perturbation run yields $\sigma_8 = 0.824$ at the fiducial parameters ($\epsilon_T = 0.0066$, $z_T = 5$, $n_T = 2$), a $+0.17\%$ enhancement relative to $\Lambda$CDM ($\sigma_8 = 0.823$) at the same cosmology — the sign is an uplift, not the suppression needed to approach the weak-lensing $S_8$. The Step 23 provenance audit (results/step_23_s8_provenance_audit.json) establishes three qualifications: (i) the MCMC posterior mean $\sigma_8 = 0.858 \pm 0.016$ is prior-boundary dominated ($A_{\rm planck}$ and $n_s$ saturate their low-$\ell$ prior ceilings; $\sigma_8$ tracks $A_{\rm planck}$ with correlation $+0.46$); (ii) the SMG alpha sector contributes zero to the matter power spectrum — the active-perturbation $P(k)$ is bitwise identical to the background-only output even at $7.6\times$ the fiducial amplitude, so all clustering modification is the scale-free Jordan-frame Hubble factor $M(z)$; and (iii) the registered $P(k)$ grids contain a $P_k^{max} = 1.0\,h/{\rm Mpc}$ boundary artifact ($+44\%$ spurious upturn at the edge, absent in an extended run) that does not affect $\sigma_8$ at the $0.02\%$ level but precludes small-scale statements from the current grids. As Stage-IV weak-lensing surveys (Euclid, Rubin/LSST) resolve their internal calibration systematics, the $S_8$ consensus will test whether any deviation from the $\Lambda$CDM growth history exists at all — the closure's prediction is a scale-free enhancement of order $0.2\%$, falsifiable in sign as well as in amplitude. Any environment-dependent (screening) contribution to weak-lensing structure growth lies outside this homogeneous linear closure by construction.

Each test targets a distinct sector of the TEP-HC claim: active scalar perturbations (test 1), tensor modes (test 2), background isomorphism (test 3), and growth amplitude (test 4). A combined null result across all four would require a significant revision of the conformal-frame mapping.
